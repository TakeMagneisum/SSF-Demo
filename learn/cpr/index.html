Emergency skills awareness

CPR & AED reference

This is a brief public awareness reference, not a certification course. Follow emergency dispatchers and use the most current instruction provided by trained responders.

First-pass educational guide · November 15, 2026

Possible cardiac arrest? If an adult is unresponsive and not breathing normally or only gasping, call 911 in the United States (or your local emergency number), send someone for an AED, and begin CPR as directed by the dispatcher. Do not delay emergency action to finish reading this webpage.

For an adult sudden collapse

Adult hands-only CPR: remember the sequence

  1. Recognize and call. Check responsiveness and normal breathing; summon emergency help and an AED.
  2. Compress. For a teen or adult sudden collapse, place hands in the center of the chest and push hard and fast, around 100–120 compressions per minute. For an average adult, AHA specifies at least 2 inches (5 cm), avoiding excessive depth greater than 2.4 inches (6 cm). Allow the chest to recoil between compressions.
  3. Use the AED. Turn it on as soon as available and follow its voice or visual prompts.
  4. Continue. Follow dispatcher instructions until qualified help takes over or the person shows signs of life.

Children, infants, and other emergencies

CPR procedures differ for children and infants, and rescue breaths are especially important in many pediatric and breathing-related emergencies. Adult hands-only guidance is not a universal protocol for babies or children. Take a current hands-on class and follow emergency dispatcher guidance. Training also covers choking, breathing emergencies, and the correct AED approach for different ages.
