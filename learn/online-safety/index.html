Personal digital security

Online safety basics

Most useful online defenses are simple habits applied consistently, especially when a message creates urgency.

First-pass educational guide · November 15, 2026

Start with four protections

  1. Use a unique password for each account. A reputable password manager can help create and store long passwords.
  2. Turn on multifactor authentication. Prefer passkeys or phishing-resistant methods when available; never share a sign-in code with a caller.
  3. Update devices and apps. Apply trusted security updates, including for your browser and phone.
  4. Pause before clicking. Visit important services through a known bookmark or address instead of a message link.

Recognize common traps

  • A fake agency or bank calls and asks for your password, code, remote access, or immediate transfer.
  • A search ad or QR code leads to a lookalike website with a slightly different address.
  • An unexpected attachment claims you must open it right away.
  • A caller insists you must keep the request secret or pay with gift cards, cryptocurrency, or a wire transfer.

If you think you were scammed

  1. Stop engaging with the suspicious caller, page, or message.
  2. If money or account access may be at risk, contact your bank or service provider through an independently verified number.
  3. Change affected passwords from a trusted device; revoke unfamiliar sessions and enable MFA.
  4. Report suspected fraud to the relevant organization and the FTC. If identity information was exposed, consider IdentityTheft.gov.
