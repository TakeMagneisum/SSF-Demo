Before you travel

Can I go today?

  • Confirm that the service can see you. Ask where to go, which entrance to use, when to arrive, and whether you need an appointment, referral, or documents.
  • Check how you’ll get there and get back. Ask what to do if you’re delayed.
“Hi, I’m trying to get to your service today. Can you confirm the arrival time and entrance? Do I need an appointment? Who should I contact if I’m late?”

Trip checklist

Choose a ride and check the whole route

Compare the bus, train, a ride arranged by the agency, and a taxi or rideshare such as Lyft or Uber where available. Ask about fare assistance before paying. For each leg, check the total cost, how to pay, transfer time, and the final walk or pickup. Check the last departure and your return trip.

If you book a rideshare, match the license plate, vehicle and driver to your booking before getting in. Confirm that the pickup point is somewhere you can wait safely.

Rideshare safety tips from Uber

Find your stop and make the transfer

Write down the route number, direction or final destination shown on the vehicle, transfer point, and exact stop name. Ask the driver for a heads-up if you’re unsure. If you miss the stop, ask where you can safely get off and reconnect. If you miss a transfer, check the next departure and call ahead if your arrival time changes.

Travel with a child or an animal

Ask each operator what you need for a baby or child, including car-seat arrangements, a stroller, and any ticket requirements. For a pet, ask about carriers, fees, and reservations. Service animals may follow different rules from pets. Confirm that both the transport operator and the receiving service can accommodate you.

US example: Amtrak travel policies

Ask for language or accessibility support

Show this line and say or point to the name of your language.

“I speak ___. I need an interpreter. Can you tell me where to go and what to do next?”

Ask whether each leg has step-free boarding, room for mobility equipment, a seat or a place to wait, and staff who can help. Keep any medication you may need accessible. Ask your pharmacist ahead of time if a delay could affect dosing or storage.

Plan for weather, delays, and a phone outage

Check official weather and operator alerts before leaving. If the road or path is flooded, stop and find a safe alternative. Write down your next stop, a safe callback number, and a staffed place to ask for help, charge your phone, or use Wi-Fi if your battery or service runs out. Keep the paper only if it is safe to carry.

US example: National Weather Service flood guidance

Plan a safe arrival at a confidential service

Ask an advocate how and where to meet, whether a safer device would help, and how to arrange the trip if someone may monitor your phone or accounts. Ask them about safe pickup, a child or pet, and a way to reach you. Leave confidential destinations off maps and printed plans.

US example: internet safety for survivors

Trip checklist

Write only what is safe to carry. Confirm intake and check travel alerts before leaving.

  • Arrival time and public entrance
  • Route, direction, and stop
  • Transfer point and next departure
  • Safe callback number
  • Backup ride or staffed place to ask for help
  • Fare, payment, and return plan
